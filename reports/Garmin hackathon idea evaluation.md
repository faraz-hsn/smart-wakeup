# Climb the stairs, then prove recovery

**Bottom line.** Build a **Stair Snack + Heart-Rate-Recovery trainer** first. **Orthostatic Morning Check** is the strongest second option, and a **Wind-Down Coach for Fenix 7-class watches** is the third. The stair app wins because it is the only candidate that is strong on all of the following at once: published evidence (stair "exercise snacks" raised VO2peak by about 7% in a 2024 RCT), no native or Connect IQ (CIQ) equivalent found, a sensor story that is real rather than decorative (barometer, accelerometer, HR, plus optional GPS/temperature), and a live demo that takes 60 seconds. The orthostatic test answers the recurring "why is my readiness low?" confusion with an active test that Polar sells and Garmin does not. It depends on optical beat-to-beat intervals, though, and those have an acknowledged bug on the Fenix 7 Pro. The Wind-Down Coach has the best *user-complaint* evidence of the three: years of bedtime-reminder requests, and Garmin shipped Evening Report and Sleep Alignment to newer watches but not the Fenix 7 Pro. Its sensor story is thin and its prompt delivery runs into CIQ's biggest limit, which is that background code cannot vibrate. The three ideas suggested earlier hold up only partly. **Stress Reset** has real demand, but breathing apps are crowded and it is hard to demo. **Smart Move Coach** has the loudest complaints of any idea, but small CIQ apps already cover scheduling, and a reminder that cannot buzz from the background is a weak reminder. **Wind-Down Coach** survives, as #3. **Smart wake for Fenix 7 owners** has the most emotional demand but is the worst one-day bet: CIQ cannot read sleep stages, and the existing smart-alarm apps are rated 2.2–2.5. One caveat applies throughout. **Reddit and X could not be fetched**, so the user evidence comes mostly from Garmin forums, reviews and papers. Spend 10 minutes checking Reddit by hand before you commit (see the last section).

## Garmin froze the Fenix 7 Pro, which sets the gap test

The gap test depends on which watch you mean. The Fenix 7 / 7 Pro gets bug fixes only. Garmin's Feb 2026 feature update skipped the series entirely, and the5krunner's Fenix 9 explainer says the 7 and 7 Pro "receive fixes only" ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/08/26/garmin-fenix-9-all-you-need-to-know/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Garmin-releases-major-update-for-older-high-end-smartwatches-with-multiple-changes-and-improvements.1387702.0.html)). So the features users asked for in 2023–2025 now exist, but on other hardware. **Smart Wake** reached Vivoactive 6, Fenix 8, Enduro 3 and Fenix 9 ([Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-2CF5620C-E585-4E0A-9CC3-9565533EEE4D/EN-US/GUID-5F030BAF-DBBA-4201-A99C-5F3E1C16CC4D.html); [Garmin Feb 2026 PDF](https://res.garmin.com/shared/emea/23957/February2026.pdf)). **Evening Report** and **Sleep Alignment** are on Fenix 8/9 and Venu 4 ([Venu 4 manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals-apac/webhelp/venu4/EN-SG/GUID-AB715A12-F0BF-442C-81A3-FAC971FE0091-1791.html); [Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2025/12/23/garmin-sleep-alignment/)). **Health Status** reaches the Fenix 7 Pro only inside the phone app ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/wearables/garmin-november-2025-update-throws-venu-4-health-status-to-more-watches)). Fenix 7 owners have noticed, and TechRadar reported a thread titled "Garmin abandoned Fenix 7 Pro after 1.5 years" ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-claim-the-company-has-abandoned-the-fenix-7-range-as-another-update-snubs-older-models)).

On the Fenix 7 Pro itself, the native wellness set looks like this. The stress alert only "notifies you when stress has drained your Body Battery". There is no user-set threshold and no Relax Reminder in its Health & Wellness menu ([Fenix 7 manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-C001C335-A8EC-4A41-AB0E-BAC434259F92/EN-US/GUID-201AB421-E901-4CCF-977B-2EAEBEB801A2.html)). The Move Alert fires after a fixed hour and is on/off only ([Fenix 7 Move Alert](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-C001C335-A8EC-4A41-AB0E-BAC434259F92/EN-US/GUID-1ED91618-3587-4DDB-A54B-ACEAC8CA3FA8.html)). The watch also has sleep score, Sleep Coach, abnormal-HR alerts, Breathwork, Health Snapshot and Pulse Ox spot checks. None of the audited health features needs Connect+, which is about AI text insights and dashboards, not on-watch interventions ([the5krunner Connect+ review](https://the5krunner.com/2026/04/20/garmin-connect-plus-review/)). The practical consequence for your pitch: an idea that fills a "Fenix 7-class gap" is honest, but judges who own a Fenix 8 may shrug. **Prefer ideas that no Garmin device does natively.** That rule favors the active tests (stair HRR, orthostatic) over the catch-up features (smart wake, evening report).

One more pattern runs through every complaint category. Users already have more data than they want, and what they complain about is **nudges that are dumb, mistimed, missed or unverifiable**. Move alerts vanish after about a second. Relax reminders fire at lunch and never during real stress. Breathing "doesn't move the needle" ([FR255 forum](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/running-multisport/f/forerunner-255-series/388121/the-move-alerts-doesn-t-work-well); [Venu 4 forum, 2026](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-ios/430901/bug---stress-alerts-set-to-during-event-in-app-and-watch-but-never-receive-them/2046629); [FR255 relax](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/running-multisport/f/forerunner-255-series/332714/relax-reminder)). A winning concept therefore follows a **closed loop**: one sensor detects the context, the watch triggers an action, and a different sensor verifies the body's response. This also turns the "bonus per sensor" rule into a story instead of a gimmick.

## Decision matrix: stair HRR leads, smart wake trails

"Sensors" counts physical sensors used for a real purpose. Stress, Body Battery and respiration are derived metrics, not sensors. The total is unweighted. If the judging is a short live demo, weight Demo, Build and Appeal ×1.5. The ranking stays the same.

The scores rest on a few judgments. **Demand** is scored from first-hand complaints, so research-only ideas such as stair snacks and the orthostatic test score low, even though the science behind them is good. **Smart Move Coach** has the most complaints of all (9+ forum threads, 2015–2026), but it loses points because CIQ apps with active hours and step-to-clear already exist, and because a reminder app's core job is to buzz you unprompted, which CIQ background code cannot do. **Smart Wake** gets a 5 for demand and native gap, but a 1 for feasibility and demo: there is no sleep-stage API, the app would have to run in the foreground all night, and you cannot show eight hours of sleep on stage. **Morning Light** is the most original idea and the most sensor-rich, but no user evidence for it surfaced, and its "outdoor" detection is an inference stack.

## Idea-by-idea assessment

### 1. Stair Snack + Heart-Rate-Recovery trainer — recommended #1

**Pitch.** "Your watch already counts floors. Ours turns the office stairwell into a fitness test." The app detects or starts a short stair bout. It measures vertical metres with the barometer and step cadence with the accelerometer, then takes a 60-second heart-rate-recovery (HRR) reading at the top. Each bout counts toward a daily dose drawn from the research: 3 bouts, about 19 m each, at least 1 h apart. Over time the user gets a personal HRR trend, a VO2-proxy they can check in two minutes without a treadmill.

**Evidence for the pain and the payoff.** The user-demand evidence is indirect, and you should say so. The loud complaint is about the dumb hourly Move Alert, which is unscheduled, fleeting and absent from native faces ([Move alert suggestion](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/423342/suggestion-move-alert); [FR255](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/running-multisport/f/forerunner-255-series/388121/the-move-alerts-doesn-t-work-well)). Reviewers also argue that hourly prompts are fooled by fidgeting ([Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/wearables/articles/those-annoying-smartwatch-move-alerts-160011132.html)). The scientific evidence is strong. Sedentary adults climbing a 3-flight stairwell 3×/day improved VO2peak ([Jenkins 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30649897/)). Three 30-second all-out bouts of 6 flights (18.9 m) raised relative VO2peak about 7%, while a 40-minute moderate cycling arm did not change significantly ([Yin 2024](https://fis.dshs-koeln.de/en/publications/exercise-snacks-are-a-time-efficient-alternative-to-moderate-inte/)). Because that protocol is defined in vertical metres, the barometric altimeter measures exactly the dose. HRR has a famous anchor: a drop of 12 bpm or less in the first minute after exercise predicted all-cause death with an adjusted RR of 2.0 ([Cole 1999, NEJM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10536127/)). Don't mix that up with the separate 42-bpm-at-2-min threshold from a 2000 cohort ([AAFP](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2000/1001/p1646.html)).

**What exists.** Natively, the Fenix 7 Pro has floors climbed and a floors goal, but no on-demand stair challenge and no standardized HRR test was found. In CIQ, no app combines barometer stair challenges with exercise snacks ([search summary](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/showcase/338907/app-step-goal-alert/1875481)). The move-reminder apps are tiny (10+ to 100+ downloads) ([Move Reminder](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/48979d17-6766-4344-baec-144d81cd3694); [Smart Move Alert](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/f50a9760-1ede-485e-8d4e-92edcbb12de5)). **Gap: wide.** One check remains: whether the Fenix 7 Pro shows a recovery-HR figure after native activities. Verify on the watch, and if it does, your differentiator is the standardized effort plus the trend, not the number itself.

**How it works.** Build it as a device app, because data fields crash on `registerSensorDataListener` and widgets time out. It reads `Sensor.Info.pressure`/`altitude` and HR at 1 Hz, and samples accelerometer cadence through `registerSensorDataListener` at up to 100 Hz, with `Math.FirFilter` and the SDK's PitchCounter sample as a starting point. A bout starts when the climb rate passes a threshold (about 12 Pa of pressure drop per metre near sea level) together with step cadence. It ends when ascent stops. The app then runs a 60-second HR countdown with a vibration at t=0 and t=60, and computes ΔHR. To log the session, use `ActivityRecording` + `FitContributor` fields (ascent m, peak HR, HRR60), so the data lands in Garmin Connect. `ActivityMonitor.Info.floorsClimbed`/`metersClimbed` work as a cross-check. A live glance (`liveUpdates: true` on the Fenix 7 Pro) can show "2/3 snacks today, last HRR 31 bpm". That avoids the background-vibrate problem entirely, because nothing needs to buzz unprompted. Optional sensor bonus: tag the bout as indoor or outdoor using a GPS fix and the temperature reading.

**Risks and mitigations.** First, optical HR lags during sharp efforts. Since HRR only needs 1 Hz HR, not beat intervals, this sidesteps the BBI bug, but present HRR as a *personal trend*, not as Cole's mortality threshold, which came from treadmill testing. Second, the barometer drifts with weather and HVAC. Use short windows (seconds to minutes) and require cadence at the same time. Third, the venue may have no stairs. Ask the organizers. If needed, record a real climb in the morning and replay it with Simulator → FIT playback. Watch for two known replay quirks: accelerometer units off by about 1000× and altitude starting at −18 m ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/399252/issue-with-fit-file-replay-and-accelerometer-units-in-simulator); [forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/410065/how-to-get-altitide-data-from-in-activity-file-to-be-usefull-in-the-ciq-simulator/1927421)). Fourth, safety: all-out stair sprints are not for everyone, so default to a "brisk" mode.

**Verdict: build this.** It has the best demo of any candidate: a teammate climbs a flight on stage or in the corridor, and the room watches metres tick up and then HR fall. It has a clean citation chain and no direct competitor. It can also absorb the legitimate part of Smart Move Coach: the glance shows hours since the last snack, which is a better answer to the move-bar complaint than another hourly buzz.

### 2. Orthostatic Morning Check — recommended #2

**Pitch.** A 2-minute morning test: lie still for 1 minute, stand up, stand still for 1 minute. The gyro and accelerometer detect the stand-up automatically. The app compares supine and standing HR, and RMSSD when beat intervals are available, against the user's own baseline, then gives a plain-language "train / easy / rest" call with the reason spelled out.

**Evidence.** Users complain that Body Battery, HRV Status and Training Readiness contradict each other with no explanation. Forum threads include "Metrics vs. Reality" and "help me understand HRV", and Garmin's own blog tells users not to decide from a single HRV reading ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/420300/garmin-devices-and-garmin-connect-android-metrics-vs-reality); [forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/instinct-2-series/311070/help-me-understand-hrv-etc/1511353); [Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-SG/blog/?p=1191)). A blogger who went back to Whoop said Garmin's data told them nothing they could act on ([jonashietala.se](https://www.jonashietala.se/blog/2025/06/02/why_im_back_to_whoop_for_now)). The science is moderate. A 2024 review says active orthostatic testing may add reactivity information for overtrained athletes, but that method-comparison studies are still needed ([Eur J Appl Physiol review](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00421-024-05601-4.pdf)). The Rusko test came from Finnish skiers, and Polar sells a commercial version ([Polar](https://www.polar.com/en/guide/orthostatic-test)).

**What exists.** Natively there is HRV Status, which is passive and overnight, and Health Snapshot, a 2-minute *still* recording of HR, stress and respiration ([Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-08EC56EE-E275-4D3C-B02A-08407D5633DB/EN-US/GUID-7E8CB930-46DE-4622-AAD0-5E21DBB1A8E1.html)). Neither is a posture-change test. In CIQ, the HRV spot-check apps (HRV Tracker, Meditate) measure resting HRV, and no orthostatic app was found ([HRV Tracker](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/3d646bc2-7dea-47d0-bc10-b52b3ef31f22)). **Gap: real, and Health Snapshot is the closest native neighbor.** Expect a judge to ask "isn't this Health Snapshot?" The answer: Snapshot is a still recording with no posture change. This test measures how the nervous system *reacts* to standing.

**How it works.** A device app, with `registerSensorDataListener` set to `:heartBeatIntervals` plus `:accelerometer`/`:gyroscope` for posture detection (pitch/roll change and the impact of standing). Compute RMSSD for each phase, plus the HR peak after standing and the settled HR. Keep a 7-day baseline in `Application.Storage`. Save to FIT with custom fields. Do **not** start an activity recording before the measurement: one developer reports beat intervals are not supplied during recording, and another says stress can't be read during recording ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/376050/heartbeatintervals-on-a-data-field); [Nap till rested](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/showcase/323051/nap-till-rested-a-power-nap-app)).

**Risks and mitigations.** The biggest risk is that the optical beat-interval callback "never called" on **Fenix 7 Pro Solar fw 20.22**. The bug is acknowledged with no fix shown, and it worked with a chest strap ([bug report](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/interval-capture-broken-again-or-not-fixed)). Test this in the first 30 minutes on your actual watch. Fallbacks: an HR-only version (standing HR rise and settle, which is still a meaningful reactivity signal), or bring a chest strap. Gyro support varies by device (it returned null on Vivoactive 5), so test it early ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/427901/vivoactive-5-gyroscope/1997362)). A one-day baseline is fake, so pre-seed some mock history for the demo and say so on stage.

**Verdict: strong second choice, or a stretch module.** It directly answers "why is my readiness low today" with a test the user performs, so they trust it more. It also shares code with idea 1 (HR capture, baseline storage, FIT fields). A "Recovery Lab" that runs both tests is tempting, but the notes flag the full composite as scope-risky for one day. Build one test fully. Add the second only if it is working by mid-afternoon.

### 3. Wind-Down Coach for Fenix 7-class watches — recommended #3 (re-checked earlier idea)

**Pitch.** Before the user's scheduled bedtime, the watch shows a wind-down prompt. It covers energy left (Body Battery), whether today's stress or a late hard workout is likely to hurt sleep, and a suggested lights-out time. It offers snooze and a 2-minute exhale-focused breathing step. Urgency rises if the user is still active, or if HR and stress stay high past the target.

**Evidence: the strongest user-complaint record of the top three.** A Fenix 7 thread asks for exactly this: an evening pop-up with energy and suggested bedtime, a "Remind me" about 15 minutes before, wind-down time, weekday/weekend targets, and configurable vibration and snooze ([Fenix 7 forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/fenix-7-series/356904/feature-request-sleep-coach-sleep-notification-and-alarm-clock)). A Connect iOS feedback post asks for a reminder at the start of the sleep window ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-ios/417936/feedback-sleep-schedule-add-optional-alarm-reminder)). A Lily 2 owner coming from Fitbit misses Fitbit's bedtime reminder ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/healthandwellness/f/lily-2-series/385631/feature-request-bedtime-reminder)). Garmin's own blog warns that late hard workouts hurt sleep, but nothing on the watch tells you in the moment ([Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-SG/blog/developing-good-sleep-habits/)).

**What exists.** On the Fenix 7 Pro there is only the informational Body Battery daily summary before the sleep window ([F7 manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-C001C335-A8EC-4A41-AB0E-BAC434259F92/EN-US/GUID-201AB421-E901-4CCF-977B-2EAEBEB801A2.html)). Evening Report and Sleep Alignment are newer-device only ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/08/26/garmin-fenix-9-all-you-need-to-know/)). Sleep Alignment is also a glance visualization, not a nagging coach, and it needs a 3-week baseline ([Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2025/12/23/garmin-sleep-alignment/)). In CIQ there is no direct competitor. Neighbors include Caffeine Tracker's "sleep-safe" time (10K+ downloads) and SleepHelper's haptic breathing ([Caffeine Tracker](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/b9325e27-582a-4aeb-a3a5-963097915714); [SleepHelper](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/showcase/428572/showcase-sleephelper-haptic-sleep-induction-sleep-vibe-built-by-a-16-year-old-dev)). **Gap: real on the Fenix 7 Pro, partial on newer watches.** The part missing everywhere is urgency that escalates with live Body Battery and stress.

**How it works.** `UserProfile.Profile.upcomingSleepTime` gives the bedtime. `Background.registerForTemporalEvent` fires at bedtime minus N minutes, and the background service reads `SensorHistory.getStressHistory`/`getBodyBatteryHistory` and today's activity (`registerForActivityCompletedEvent` for late workouts). Delivery is the hard part. Background code **cannot vibrate**, so the prompt must come through `Notifications.showNotification` (API 5.1, Fenix 7 Pro listed, on-device background behavior unverified) or `requestApplicationWake`. The latter shows a confirm dialog, reportedly with no buzz ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/445482/can-a-connect-iq-app-sound-an-alert-from-the-background); [Notifications bugs](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/instinct-3-solar-45mm-notifications-shownotification-from-a-background-service-shows-nothing-works-in-foreground)). A live glance with a bedtime countdown works regardless. Once the user opens the app, the foreground breathing step can vibrate and show HR falling.

**Risks and mitigations.** Delivery may silently fail on the real watch, so test `showNotification` from a background temporal event in hour one. If it fails, pitch the glance + "tap to wind down" flow honestly. The sensor story is thin (HR, accelerometer, and derived stress/Body Battery). You can add temperature (bedroom-climate hint, since evidence puts sleep efficiency at its peak around 20–25 °C ([Baniassadi 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37474050/))), but the wrist sensor is skin-biased, so frame it as a hint. Judges with newer Garmins may say "Evening Report does this", so lead with escalation and the closed loop, not "a reminder".

**Verdict: build it if your team values user evidence over sensor points.** It is the most defensible idea when someone asks "does anyone actually want this?" It is weaker on the sensor bonus and on stage drama. It is a good fit if the team wants a pure sleep/recovery story.

### 4. Morning Light Quest / Desk-to-Daylight Break

**Pitch.** Get outside within the first hour after waking, and take daytime breaks outdoors. Breaks count double outside and triple on stairs. "Outside" is inferred from a GPS fix plus a temperature shift plus steps.

**Evidence.** The science is associative only. Morning sunlight is linked to an earlier sleep midpoint ([PsyPost on BMC study](https://www.psypost.org/morning-sunlight-exposure-is-linked-to-earlier-sleep-schedules/)). Light after waking is linked to less sleep inertia ([medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.10.21.20214676v3.full.pdf)). About 120 min/week in nature is associated with better wellbeing ([White 2019](https://read.qxmd.com/read/31197192/spending-at-least-120-minutes-a-week-in-nature-is-associated-with-good-health-and-wellbeing)). **No user complaints or wishes about daylight surfaced at all.**

**What exists.** Nothing, natively or in CIQ, as far as the research found. **Gap: wide open, but possibly because few people asked.**

**How it works.** `Position` for the GPS fix (a sky-view proxy), `Sensor.Info.temperature`, steps, and `UserProfile.wakeTime`. One untested bonus: the SDK lists a `SOLAR_INPUT` complication, and the simulator config marks the Fenix 7 Pro as solar-capable. If it returns live solar intensity on a Solar variant, it is a crude light sensor, and that would make this idea much stronger.

**Risks.** GPS fixes work through windows and fail in urban canyons. Wrist temperature reflects skin, not the air. Without the solar trick there is no lux measurement. It is hard to demo indoors.

**Verdict: the dark horse.** It is the most original idea and uses a lot of sensors, but the demand case is weak. Check `SOLAR_INPUT` for 5 minutes. If it gives real values, reconsider this idea, or bolt "outdoor bout ×2" onto the stair app.

### 5. Hyperfocus / Focus Guard

**Pitch.** A haptic focus timer that also watches the body. Long wrist stillness plus a rising HR triggers a movement or breathing break, and gentle "time passing" pulses help with time blindness.

**Evidence.** Garmin has no native focus mode. A plain CIQ Pomodoro has **100K+ downloads** (rated 4.0 from 203 reviews), which proves demand for focus timers ([Pomodoro](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/4b11ad8f-3e48-4112-83df-336065c49829)). The ADHD angle rests on affiliate listicles and iOS apps, not Garmin users ([example](https://brianvanderwaal.com/best-adhd-watch/)). Wrist-motion stress detection degrades from 93.5% offline to 77.8% in real time ([StresSense](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/39281042)).

**What exists.** Many Pomodoro apps, whose top complaint is "doesn't work in the background", plus Pomodoro Sense, a stress-adaptive break prototype ([Pomodoro Sense](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fariosavi%2Fpomodoro-sense)). **Gap: the biometric part only.**

**Risks.** The app must stay in the foreground for the whole session. Its HR-rise signal at a desk is weak and noisy, and the "I detected hyperfocus" moment is hard to stage convincingly.

**Verdict: buildable, but low distinction.** Pick it only if a teammate is personally passionate about ADHD and can tell that story.

### 6. Smart Move Coach (re-checked earlier idea)

**Pitch.** Replace the fixed hourly move alert with work-hours scheduling, an alert that stays until dismissed, a reset that needs real steps or an HR rise, and suggested exercise snacks counted by the accelerometer.

**Evidence: the strongest of any idea.** It is the most complained-about everyday feature on the Garmin forums, across 9+ threads from about 2015 to 2026. Users ask for active hours, weekday-only alerts, custom intervals, and an alert that doesn't vanish after about 1 second ([Move alert suggestion, replies ~Mar 2026](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/423342/suggestion-move-alert); [Venu 3](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/healthandwellness/f/venu-3-series/378134/venu-3---hourly-activity-move-reminder); [FR945](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/running-multisport/f/forerunner-945/268802/how-to-make-move-alerts-more-like-fitbit)). A pre-post study of 87 office workers found about 43 fewer sitting minutes per day with a break-reminder app, though it had no control group ([PubMed 41877101](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41877101/)). The caveat: office workers in a co-design study questioned whether time-based prompts help or just add stress ([PMC13075341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13075341/)).

**What exists.** Native: the fixed move bar. CIQ: **Move Reminder** (10–120 min interval, active hours), **Smart Move Alert** (cleared only after N steps) and **Customize Move Alert** (interval, step goal, quiet hours) ([Move Reminder](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/48979d17-6766-4344-baec-144d81cd3694); [Smart Move Alert](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/f50a9760-1ede-485e-8d4e-92edcbb12de5); [Customize Move Alert](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/faff1a29-1f53-4018-ad7f-4d321e9309a4)). They are small, but they exist, so "scheduling" is **not** an unsolved gap. What remains is verified completion with exercise counting and stairs, and nobody combines those.

**The technical catch.** A reminder app is only as good as its buzz. CIQ background services cannot vibrate, and the API 5.1 Notifications path has acknowledged on-device failures on other models ([CIQQA-4848](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/instinct-3-solar-45mm-notifications-shownotification-from-a-background-service-shows-nothing-works-in-foreground); [CIQQA-4699](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/notifications-shownotification-renders-in-venu-3s-simulator-but-not-on-physical-venu-3s)). The existing apps work around this by running as a foreground device app, which is why they are clunky.

**Verdict: don't build it as a reminder app.** Fold its valid insight ("make the move break real and verified") into the Stair Snack trainer.

### 7. Stress Reset (re-checked earlier idea)

**Pitch.** A user-tuned stress trigger. The user sets the threshold and duration, and alerts are suppressed when the accelerometer shows motion, so exercise doesn't count as stress. A yes/no "do you feel stressed?" check calibrates the threshold. Then comes guided breathing with live HR shown dropping as proof.

**Evidence: solid and recent.** A Venu 4 owner has never received a relax reminder despite hours of high stress. Support "can't reproduce", a second user reports the same on an FR965, and Garmin documents no threshold (thread ~Feb–Aug 2026) ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-ios/430901/bug---stress-alerts-set-to-during-event-in-app-and-watch-but-never-receive-them/2046629)). Older complaints: alerts fire while calm ([Vivoactive 4](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/healthandwellness/f/vivoactive-4-series/329799/vivoactive-4-relax-reminder)), and breathing doesn't lower the reading ([FR255](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/running-multisport/f/forerunner-255-series/332714/relax-reminder)). Native Breathwork's before/after is distrusted because stress starts at 0 ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/323264/breathwork-activities-don-t-track-stress-correctly)). The intervention science is good: HRV biofeedback had a large pre-post effect on stress and anxiety ([Goessl 2017](https://open.bu.edu/handle/2144/26911)), and 5 minutes a day of cyclic sighing beat mindfulness for mood ([Balban 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)). Two counterpoints are worth knowing before a judge raises them. A 781-student study found Garmin stress did not match self-reported stress ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Smartwatch-tech-fails-to-measure-stress-in-new-study.1088069.0.html)). Marco Altini also notes that slow breathing lowers an HRV-based stress score by construction, so "verified drop" is partly circular ([Altini](https://marcoaltini.substack.com/p/pitfalls-of-estimates/comments)).

**What exists.** Native on the Fenix 7 Pro: a stress alert based on Body Battery drain, and Breathwork. CIQ: **Meditate** (HRV, stress, respiration; 10K+ downloads, rated 4.6, no longer maintained), **Breathing Timer** (paced programs with RMSSD or RSA fallback), HRV Tracker, and Nap till rested, which ends a nap on a stress threshold ([Meditate](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/c5fc5ea5-7d12-4fb9-be9c-701663a39db7); [Breathing Timer](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/59828c15-7638-4e14-b871-47f0ce0c66f0)). **Gap: only the automatic trigger + calibration + verification loop.** "Breathing after a stress alert" on its own is solved.

**Technical reality.** `ActivityMonitor.Info.stressScore` (API 5.0, 30-second rolling average) and `getStressHistory` are readable. But detection happens in a 5-minute background poll that cannot buzz, so the trigger is the weakest link again. Verification needs beat intervals, which are buggy on the Fenix 7 Pro, so plain HR drop is the fallback. The simulator does not simulate stress values, so the demo needs injected data.

**Verdict: real need, poor hackathon fit.** You cannot produce a genuine stress spike on stage, and the judges have probably seen breathing apps. If you love it, demo it as user-launched: "I feel stressed → 90 s cyclic sighing → HR −8 bpm, respiration down". Be explicit that auto-triggering is the roadmap.

### 8. Resonance Finder

**Pitch.** Sweep breathing pace from 6.5 down to 4.5 breaths/min and find the user's personal resonance pace, where HR oscillation is largest. Reported averages sit around 5.5/min rather than the usual 6 ([AAPB/Lehrer](https://aapb.starchapter.com/images/meta_analysis_andstatus_of_field_.pdf)). Daily sessions then run at that pace.

**Verdict.** It is a genuine differentiator over fixed patterns, but it needs minutes of steady data per step (slow to demo) and clean beat intervals (buggy). It is best used as a feature inside Stress Reset, not as a standalone entry.

### 9. Smart Wake for Fenix 7 owners

**Pitch.** Give Fenix 7 owners the smart-wake alarm Garmin withheld.

**Evidence: emotionally the strongest of all.** It is "one of the most-requested features", and owners complain about segmentation and feeling abandoned ([Fenix 7 forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/fenix-7-series/352090/feature-request-smart-wake-up-alarm/1926438); [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-claim-the-company-has-abandoned-the-fenix-7-range-as-another-update-snubs-older-models)).

**Why it fails anyway.** CIQ "doesn't provide access to native sleep stage data", and background tasks run only a few seconds every 5 minutes and cannot vibrate ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/app-ideas/390116/new-sleep-management-and-smart-alarm-app)). Any smart alarm must therefore run as a foreground app all night on its own motion/HR heuristics. That is exactly why **SmartWake PRO (50K+, rated 2.5)** and **Sleep as Android HRV (100K+, rated 2.2)** score so poorly ([SmartWake PRO](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/86f765e4-0bf3-4401-a503-058ddcfb56d2); [Sleep as Android](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/e80a4793-f5a3-44c7-bd7f-52a97f5d8310)). Garmin's own sleep staging only agrees with reference about 40–50% of the time ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/10/27/scientist-finds-garmin-accuracy-bad-for-sleep-stage-identification/)), so a homemade heuristic will do no better. You also can't demo a night's sleep.

**Verdict: avoid.** The demand is real, but the platform defeats it. Big incumbents already fail at it, and the native feature exists on every current Garmin flagship.

## Fenix 7 Pro constraints cheat-sheet

All values come from the local SDK 9.1.0 docs and the `fenix7pro` device files unless a link is given.

| Area | Limit / fact | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| App type | Device (watch) app 768 KB. Glance 64 KB. Background 64 KB. Data field 256 KB. Watch face 128 KB | Build a **device app**, which has full sensor access. Data fields crash on `registerSensorDataListener`. Watch faces can't use GPS, compass or other sensors |
| High-rate sensors | Accel/gyro up to 100 Hz, magnetometer 50 Hz. `:period` max 4 s. Only one listener registration at a time. Magnetometer not in synchronous mode | Register accel+gyro+HR intervals in **one** call. Filter with `Math.FirFilter`/`IirFilter` |
| Beat intervals | `heartBeatIntervals` supported, but the optical callback "never called" on **Fenix 7 Pro Solar fw 20.22** (acknowledged, no fix shown) ([bug](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/interval-capture-broken-again-or-not-fixed)). Empty arrays can cause out-of-bounds crashes | Test in hour one. Fall back to 1 Hz HR or a chest strap. Guard empty arrays |
| Recording side-effects | Reportedly no beat intervals and no stress readings while an activity is recording ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/376050/heartbeatintervals-on-a-data-field); [Nap till rested](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/showcase/323051/nap-till-rested-a-power-nap-app)) | Measure first, record FIT afterwards, or verify on the device |
| Health metrics | `ActivityMonitor.Info.stressScore` (API 5.0, 30 s rolling), `respirationRate`, `timeToRecovery`, floors/metres climbed, `moveBarLevel`. `SensorHistory` has stress, Body Battery, HR, SpO2, pressure, elevation, temperature. **No HRV, no sleep stages, no sleep score** anywhere in the API | Any HRV must be computed yourself from beat intervals. Sleep-stage ideas are out |
| Other sensors | `Sensor.Info`: pressure, altitude, heading, mag, temperature, oxygenSaturation. Every field may be null | Null-check everything. Whether SpO2/temperature populate live outside native spot checks is unverified |
| Background | Temporal events every ≥5 min, ≤30 s run time, 64 KB, can be killed anytime. **Cannot vibrate or sound** ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/445482/can-a-connect-iq-app-sound-an-alert-from-the-background)). Triggers: temporal, sleep/wake time, steps, goal, activity completed | Don't design anything that depends on an unprompted buzz |
| Proactive alerts | `requestApplicationWake` (confirm dialog, reportedly no buzz). `Notifications.showNotification` (API 5.1, Fenix 7 Pro listed). Background display/vibe bugs on Instinct 3, Venu 3S, FR955 | Test `showNotification` from a background event on the real watch before relying on it |
| Glance | `liveUpdates: true`, 171×63 px area, keep updates under 1 Hz | A live glance is your always-visible status and needs no notification |
| Profile / context | `upcomingSleepTime`/`upcomingWakeTime`, resting HR, VO2max, weight/height/birth year. `Weather` (may not match the current location) | Bedtime-relative scheduling is easy |
| Storage / output | 10 MB app storage. `FitContributor` custom fields (records ≤1/s) sync to Garmin Connect | Persist baselines. Show results in Connect for extra credibility |
| Simulator | "Simulate Data" gives random values. FIT playback works (press play). Accel units can be about 1000× off, altitude starts at −18 m. No documented stress/Body Battery simulation | Record real FIT files in the morning for replay. Mock stress/Body Battery history in code |

## Verify on a real watch, ask the organizers, and check Reddit

Settle these on the actual Fenix 7 Pro in the first hour. They change what you can build. **(1)** Do optical `heartBeatIntervals` arrive on the current firmware? Stable 27.18 / beta 28.02 are bug-fix releases, and the reported bug was on 20.22. **(2)** Does `Notifications.showNotification` called from a background temporal event appear *and vibrate*? **(3)** Does the gyroscope stream return data, or nulls? **(4)** Are `Sensor.Info.oxygenSaturation` and `temperature` populated live? **(5)** Does a `SOLAR_INPUT` complication return a changing value on a Solar unit, i.e. can it serve as a light proxy? **(6)** How noisy is the barometer over one flight of stairs indoors? **(7)** Can stress be read while an activity is recording? **(8)** Does the watch have a Morning Report? The sources conflict ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/08/26/garmin-fenix-9-all-you-need-to-know/) says no). **(9)** Does the native activity summary already show a recovery-HR value?

Ask the organizers: the exact rubric and how much the sensor bonus weighs. What counts as "real use" of a sensor (is reading `altitude` enough, or must it drive a decision?). Whether the demo can run in the simulator or must be on a real watch. Whether watches and chest straps are provided. Whether a phone companion or web backend is allowed. Whether there are stairs near the stage. Whether judges favor ideas only for the Fenix 7 Pro or ones that run on current hardware.

The evidence has known limits. **Reddit and X could not be fetched at all.** Every attempt returned no Reddit URLs or failed, so Reddit sentiment appears only second-hand, through journalists and one unverified search snippet. The first-hand voices are Garmin forum posters, who are self-selected complainers. "Frequency" means the number of distinct threads found, not a sample. No public Garmin hackathon rubric or winner list was found. Spend **10 minutes on Reddit** (r/Garmin, r/fenix, r/GarminFenix) searching "move alert", "bedtime reminder", "smart wake fenix 7", "relax reminder", "stairs" and "orthostatic". If the stair or orthostatic searches return real enthusiasm, your top two get firmer. If they are silent while "bedtime reminder" is loud, promote the Wind-Down Coach.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "What's the most-wanted unsolved Garmin feature?" has a discouraging answer: the most-wanted features (smart wake, a schedulable move alert, a bedtime reminder) are either already on newer Garmins, already in small CIQ apps, or blocked by CIQ's no-background-buzz rule. The better question is **"what can a watch app do in the foreground that Garmin's always-on firmware does not?"** The answer is *active tests*: short, user-initiated protocols where sensors measure a standardized effort and the body's response. Stair HRR and the orthostatic check are both of that type. They sidestep the background limits entirely, they produce a number the user earned and therefore trusts, and they turn the sensor bonus into a measurement chain a judge can follow.

The honest weakness of the top pick is that nobody on the forums is asking for a stair HRR test. Its case rests on research and novelty, not on complaints. Pitch it that way: the complaint is "my move alert is dumb and my readiness score is a black box", and the answer is a 2-minute test that tells you how fit and how recovered you are, verified by three sensors.

## Final ranking: our top 3 and why

We judged every idea against the same seven questions:
- Does anyone actually want it?
- Does Garmin already do it on the Fenix 7 Pro?
- Does a Connect IQ app already do it?
- Can the platform actually do it?
- How many sensors does it use for real?
- Can we show it live?
- Can we build it in one day?

The three ideas below are the only ones that pass all seven without a fatal "no". The ones that failed did so for clear reasons:
- **Smart wake:** the platform can't read sleep stages, and you can't demo a night's sleep.
- **Move reminder:** existing apps already do the scheduling, and background code can't buzz you.
- **Stress Reset:** crowded with breathing apps, and you can't stage a real stress spike.

### #1 — Stair Snack + Heart-Rate-Recovery trainer (33/40)

**Why it's first:** it is the only idea that scores 4 or 5 on gap, demo, buildability and judging appeal at the same time. Every sensor in it drives a decision.

- **The science is strong and specific.**
  - **Yin 2024:** three 30-second all-out climbs of about 19 m raised VO2peak by about 7%. A 40-minute moderate cycling session did not change it significantly ([Yin 2024](https://fis.dshs-koeln.de/en/publications/exercise-snacks-are-a-time-efficient-alternative-to-moderate-inte/)).
  - **Jenkins 2019:** an earlier trial found 3-flight stair climbs, three times a day, improved fitness in sedentary adults ([Jenkins 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30649897/)).
  - **Cole 1999 (NEJM):** heart rate dropping by 12 bpm or less in the first minute after exercise predicted death, with roughly double the risk ([Cole 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10536127/)).
- **The sensors are the protocol, not decoration.** The research doses stair climbing in vertical metres, which is exactly what the barometer measures. The accelerometer confirms the steps are real climbing and not a lift ride or a weather change. The optical heart rate gives the 60-second recovery number. GPS and temperature can optionally tag indoor or outdoor.
- **Nothing like it exists.**
  - **Natively,** the Fenix 7 Pro only counts floors. It has no stair challenge and no standard recovery test ([Fenix 7 manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-C001C335-A8EC-4A41-AB0E-BAC434259F92/EN-US/GUID-201AB421-E901-4CCF-977B-2EAEBEB801A2.html)).
  - **In the Connect IQ store,** the only move-related apps are tiny reminders with 10 to 100+ downloads, and none combines a barometer, an exercise snack and a recovery test ([Move Reminder](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/48979d17-6766-4344-baec-144d81cd3694); [Smart Move Alert](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/f50a9760-1ede-485e-8d4e-92edcbb12de5)).
- **It avoids every platform trap we found.** The user starts it, so it needs no background buzz. It needs only 1 Hz heart rate, so the Fenix 7 Pro beat-interval bug doesn't matter ([bug report](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/interval-capture-broken-again-or-not-fixed)). It runs as a foreground device app, which has full sensor access.
- **It answers a real complaint, indirectly.** The loudest everyday complaint we found, with 9+ forum threads from 2015 to 2026, is that the move alert is dumb: unscheduled, gone in a second, and reset by fidgeting ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/423342/suggestion-move-alert); [FR255](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/running-multisport/f/forerunner-255-series/388121/the-move-alerts-doesn-t-work-well)). This app replaces "get up for a minute" with a 60-second effort that is checked and actually improves fitness.
- **It has the best demo.** A teammate climbs one flight, the metres tick up, and heart rate falls on screen within 60 seconds. As a backup, replay a FIT file recorded that morning in the simulator.
- **Honest weakness:** no user is asking for this exact app. Pitch it as the answer to "my move alert is dumb and I don't know how fit I am", not as a requested feature.

### #2 — Orthostatic Morning Check (28/40)

**Why it's second:** it turns the most confusing part of the Garmin experience, readiness numbers that contradict each other, into a 2-minute test the user does themselves and can understand.

- **The complaint is real and recent.** Users report Body Battery, HRV Status and Training Readiness disagreeing with no explanation ([Metrics vs. Reality](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/420300/garmin-devices-and-garmin-connect-android-metrics-vs-reality); [help me understand HRV](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/instinct-2-series/311070/help-me-understand-hrv-etc/1511353)). Garmin's own blog says not to decide from a single HRV reading ([Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-SG/blog/?p=1191)). A 2025 blogger went back to Whoop because Garmin's data told them nothing they could act on ([jonashietala.se](https://www.jonashietala.se/blog/2025/06/02/why_im_back_to_whoop_for_now)).
- **The method is established.** The lie-then-stand test comes from Finnish endurance sports and is sold commercially by Polar ([Polar](https://www.polar.com/en/guide/orthostatic-test)). A 2024 review says it adds information on how the body reacts to load, though more validation is needed ([review](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00421-024-05601-4.pdf)).
- **Garmin doesn't have it.** Health Snapshot is a 2-minute recording while sitting still, with no change of posture ([Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-08EC56EE-E275-4D3C-B02A-08407D5633DB/EN-US/GUID-7E8CB930-46DE-4622-AAD0-5E21DBB1A8E1.html)). HRV Status is passive and overnight. No orthostatic Connect IQ app was found.
- **The sensors have real jobs.** The gyroscope and accelerometer detect the moment you stand. Heart rate and beat intervals measure how your body reacts. The verdict is plain language: train, take it easy, or rest.
- **Why it's not #1:** it depends on optical beat intervals, which have an acknowledged Fenix 7 Pro bug ([bug report](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/interval-capture-broken-again-or-not-fixed)). A meaningful baseline also takes days, so the demo needs pre-seeded history. If beat intervals fail, the fallbacks are a heart-rate-only version or a chest strap.

### #3 — Wind-Down Coach for Fenix 7-class watches (27/40)

**Why it's third:** it has the strongest direct user demand of the three, but it uses fewer sensors and runs into the platform's biggest limit.

- **Users have asked for it for years.**
  - **Fenix 7 owners** asked for an evening pop-up with energy left, a suggested bedtime, a "remind me" 15 minutes before, and snooze ([Fenix 7 forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/fenix-7-series/356904/feature-request-sleep-coach-sleep-notification-and-alarm-clock)).
  - **A Connect iOS post** asks for a reminder at the start of the sleep window ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-ios/417936/feedback-sleep-schedule-add-optional-alarm-reminder)).
  - **Ex-Fitbit users** miss Fitbit's bedtime reminder ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/healthandwellness/f/lily-2-series/385631/feature-request-bedtime-reminder)).
- **Garmin solved it only on newer watches.** Evening Report and Sleep Alignment are on Fenix 8/9 and Venu 4, but not the Fenix 7 Pro, which now gets bug fixes only ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/08/26/garmin-fenix-9-all-you-need-to-know/); [Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2025/12/23/garmin-sleep-alignment/)). Fenix 7 owners publicly feel abandoned ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-claim-the-company-has-abandoned-the-fenix-7-range-as-another-update-snubs-older-models)). No Connect IQ app does this.
- **The part missing everywhere:** urgency that rises with live Body Battery, stress and a late hard workout. Garmin's own blog says late hard workouts hurt sleep, yet nothing on the watch warns you ([Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-SG/blog/developing-good-sleep-habits/)).
- **Why it's not higher:** it uses fewer sensors (HR, accelerometer, plus derived stress and Body Battery). Background code can't vibrate, so the bedtime nudge must rely on `showNotification`, which has unverified on-device behaviour, or on a glance ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/445482/can-a-connect-iq-app-sound-an-alert-from-the-background)). Judges with a Fenix 8 may also say "Evening Report already does this".

### How to choose between them

- **Most points from judges and a live demo:** pick #1.
- **The recovery story:** pick #2, but only if beat intervals work on the team's watch.
- **The strongest "people actually asked for this" story:** pick #3, and accept fewer sensor points.

#1 and #2 share most of their code (heart-rate capture, baseline storage, FIT fields). So the safest plan is to build #1 fully and add #2 as a second test only if #1 works by mid-afternoon.
